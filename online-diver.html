Course Modules

19 rigorous modules approved by our internal standards committee (Dave, after two beers)

Module 1: Breathing (In Theory)
Method: YouTube video • Duration: 6 minutes
This module teaches you the fundamental concept of breathing underwater without the inconvenience of being underwater. Watch Dave breathe into his webcam for 6 minutes while explaining the "inhale-exhale cycle" like it's rocket science. You will practice by breathing along with the video. Dave will not know if you're doing it right. That's between you and your lungs.
🎯 Passing Criteria: You must breathe continuously for the duration of the video. If you stop breathing, you fail. (If you stop breathing for any other reason, please seek immediate medical attention.)
🤝
Module 2: Buddy Breathing via Zoom
Method: Breakout rooms • Duration: Until one of you gives up
Find a partner. Enter a Zoom breakout room. Both of you breathe. Now share a single breath. Via the screen. We call this "conceptual regulator sharing." The exercise builds trust, eye contact, and a deep understanding that neither of you is receiving any actual air from this transaction. Advanced practitioners attempt to inhale and exhale in perfect unison. This is called "rhythmic buddy breathing" and it is entirely made up.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Maintain eye contact with your buddy on screen for 30 seconds without laughing. Smiling is acceptable. Laughing indicates you are taking the simulated emergency too lightly.

Module 3: Controlled Emergency Swimming Ascent (CESA)
Method: Simulated ascent via standing up • Duration: 30 seconds
You're out of air at 30 feet. What do you do? In the real world, you'd make a controlled ascent while exhaling continuously. In PODI Online Diver, you stand up from your chair as quickly as possible while making a continuous "ahhhhh" sound. The sound simulates the exhale. The standing simulates the ascent. If you stand up so fast you get lightheaded, that's "simulated hypoxia." Keep going. Real divers push through.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Stand up from seated position in under 3 seconds while maintaining audible exhale. Feet must leave the floor briefly (this is the "swimming" part). Video submission required. No verticality, no certification.
💧
Module 4: Mask Clearing (Dry Version)
Method: Webcam-assisted eye contact • Duration: 4 minutes
In traditional scuba, mask clearing involves flooding your mask with water and blowing it out through your nose. In PODI Online Diver, you stare into your webcam while Dave counts to 30 without blinking. The "flood" is metaphorical. The "clear" is the moment you remember this is a real certification. If your eyes water from staring, that counts as "mask flooding." Blinking is "clearing." You are now a mask-clearing expert.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Maintain unbroken eye contact with webcam for 30 seconds. No blinking allowed. If you blink, the mask is "flooded" and you must restart. We recommend setting up a fan to simulate "underwater currents" across your face.
⚖️
Module 5: Buoyancy Control (Chair-Based)
Method: Posture adjustment • Duration: 8 minutes
Buoyancy is the art of hovering effortlessly in the water column. We teach it using the PODI Chair Buoyancy System (patent pending). Lean forward slightly — this is "descending." Lean back — "ascending." Find the perfect middle position where you are neither leaning forward nor back — that is "neutral buoyancy." Hold this position for 60 seconds while Dave times you. If you wobble, you "silted out." If you fall off the chair, you "experienced an uncontrolled ascent." Pass the chair, pass the course.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Hold neutral position for 60 seconds. No arm flailing. No foot touching the floor. If your chair has wheels, it's on "hard mode."
👂
Module 6: Equalization (Desktop Barotrauma Avoidance)
Method: Yawning on command • Duration: 3 minutes
Equalizing is the act of clearing your ears as you descend. In PODI Online Diver, we teach the Valsalva maneuver by having you pinch your nose and blow gently while staring at a picture of a fish getting deeper. The photo gets progressively darker to simulate depth. If you feel pressure in your ears, you're doing it right. If you feel nothing, you are either naturally equalized or clinically dead. Either way, move on to the next module.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Successfully yawn or feel your ears pop within 5 attempts. If you cannot yawn on command, you are "equalization-challenged" and may require the "advanced yawn" workshop (+$49).
🧭
Module 7: Underwater Navigation (Desktop Compass)
Method: Google Maps Street View • Duration: 15 minutes
Navigation underwater is critical. We use Google Maps Street View with the blue dot. Spin around in your chair. The blue dot moves. Congratulations — you just navigated. For the advanced portion, close your eyes, spin your chair three times, and point in the direction you think is "back to the boat." This is called "kinesthetic dive navigation" and we made it up five minutes ago.
🎯 Passing Criteria: After spinning three times, you must point within 90 degrees of your starting orientation. Use a landmark in your room as "the boat." If you knock anything over, you "entered the wreck." Proceed carefully.
🏳️
Module 8: Surface Marker Buoy Deployment
Method: Inflating a balloon • Duration: Until you get dizzy
A Surface Marker Buoy (SMB) signals your position to the boat. We use a party balloon. Inflate it in one continuous breath (this simulates a DSMB spool). Tie it off. Attach a string. This is your "line." If the balloon floats to your ceiling, you have successfully "deployed." If it pops, you "experienced a catastrophic buoy failure at depth." Retrieve the pieces and try again. Real divers learn from failure. You will learn from latex fragments.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Balloon must remain inflated and ceiling-bound for at least 60 seconds. Selfie with your deployed SMB required for certification records. No filter. We need to see the shame in your eyes.

Module 10: Buddy Check (Self-Administered)
Method: Mirror check • Duration: 2 minutes
Find a mirror. Look at yourself. This is your "buddy." Check that your "buddy" has all required equipment. Does your buddy look ready? Does your buddy feel ready? Ask your buddy (out loud). If your buddy responds, you are either hallucinating from nitrogen narcosis or there is someone behind you. Either way, you pass. The buddy system is about trust. Trust that you remembered everything. Trust that you know what you're doing. The mirror knows the truth. The mirror always knows.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Audibly say "buddy check complete" to your reflection. Record this moment. We will review it. We will judge you silently.

Module 11: Rescue Diver (Lateral)
Method: CPR on a pillow • Duration: 10 minutes
Your buddy is unresponsive on the surface. What do you do? In PODI Online Diver, you perform rescue breaths on a pillow. Any pillow. Your sofa cushion. A stuffed animal. A stack of laundry. The material doesn't matter — the intent does. Tilt the head (estimate). Lift the chin (guess). Give two rescue breaths into the fabric. Continue for 10 minutes or until someone walks in and asks what you're doing. The embarrassment is part of the realism.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Perform 3 cycles of 30 compressions and 2 breaths on your pillow. Compressions should be to the beat of "Stayin' Alive" by the Bee Gees. If you choose a different song, you must submit a written explanation.

Module 12: Emergency Ascent (Panic Edition)
Method: Stair sprint • Duration: As fast as possible
Sometimes you need to get to the surface NOW. We simulate this by having you sprint up and down a flight of stairs (or if no stairs, run in place aggressively). The rapid breathing mimics the "panic response." The stair climbing mimics the "ascent." If you trip, you "hit your head on the boat." If you fall, you "lost consciousness at depth." Finish the sprint. The surface is waiting. So is your certificate.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Complete 3 flights of stairs (up and down) in under 45 seconds. Video proof required. Heavy breathing encouraged. Crying not disqualifying.

Module 13: Dive Computer Operation (App Edition)
Method: A stopwatch app • Duration: You set the timer
Dive computers are essential for safe diving. Ours is an app. Open your phone's stopwatch. Start it. This is your "dive." Watch the numbers go up. This is your "depth." When you feel the timer has been going for "long enough," stop it. That's your "bottom time." Subtract 5 minutes for "decompression." Whatever remains is your "actual dive time." Send a screenshot to Dave. He will enter it in our database. The database is a Google Sheet. Dave has not checked it since 2023.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Start and stop the timer. Screenshot the result. If the screenshot includes any other app (TikTok, Instagram, etc.), you are "multitasking" and receive bonus credit for "situational awareness."

Module 14: Current Awareness (Weather Channel)
Method: Watching your local news • Duration: One full broadcast
Understanding ocean conditions is critical. Tune into your local weather forecast. If the weather person mentions "wind," that's "surface chop." If they mention "waves," that's "swell." If they mention "hurricane," that's "advanced conditions." Write down the marine forecast in a notebook. This is your "dive briefing." If there is no marine forecast, write "conditions acceptable" three times. You have now completed a pre-dive environment assessment.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Submit a written weather report for your area. If the report predicts "partly cloudy" or better, you are cleared for "diving." If it predicts rain, you "dove in the rain." Real divers don't cancel for weather. Neither do we.

Module 14a: Fin Pivot (Swivel Chair Edition)
Method: Chair rotation • Duration: 90 seconds of controlled spinning
The fin pivot is a foundational skill where you balance on your fin tips without touching the bottom. Ours is on a swivel chair. Rotate exactly 360 degrees without using your feet. If you overshoot, you "lost buoyancy." If you undershoot, you "hit the reef." If you fall off, you "silted out the entire dive site." Swivel chair skill is directly transferable to underwater environments according to our internal physics department (Kyle watched a YouTube video about gyroscopes once).
🎯 Passing Criteria: Complete one full rotation without touching the floor with your feet. Counter-clockwise is easier. Clockwise is advanced. Non-swivel chairs receive a syllabus exemption but must write a 200-word essay on "What I Learned About Rotation From This Module."

Module 14b: Regulator Recovery (Pen Behind Ear)
Method: The sweep technique • Duration: 2 minutes
Losing your regulator underwater is a critical skill to recover from. We simulate this by placing a pen behind your ear and then "forgetting" where it is. Lean your head to the right, reach back with your right arm in a wide sweeping arc, and retrieve the pen. This is the exact same muscle memory used for real regulator recovery. Now do the left side. It's not there. That's because you didn't put a pen behind your left ear. You weren't paying attention. Divers pay attention. Try again.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Retrieve pen from behind ear on first attempt. If pen falls, you "lost your reg in silt" and must do 5 more sweeps eyes closed. If you still can't find it, the pen is "lost to the deep." You are now "out of air." Complete Module 3 (CESA) immediately.

Module 14c: Weight Belt Removal & Replacement
Method: Taking a backpack on and off • Duration: 45 seconds
At the surface, you may need to remove your weight belt to pass it to the boat. We simulate this using a backpack containing anything heavy (books, gaming laptop, emotional baggage). Remove the backpack. Hand it to an imaginary boat attendant ("Dave" — not the instructor, a different Dave). The imaginary Dave will take it and say nothing because imaginary Dave is professional. Now put the backpack back on without dropping it. Real weight belts don't have shoulder straps, but this is online, so close enough.
🎯 Passing Criteria: Remove and replace backpack in under 45 seconds. If you fail, the imaginary boat attendant dropped your weight belt. It's now at the bottom. You are now "unweighted." You will "surface uncontrollably." This is fine. Just stand up. You were sitting the whole time.

Blue Hole — Dahab, Egypt

Dahab's Blue Hole is legendary, mainly because of the "Arch" — a 26-meter-long tunnel at 55 meters depth that connects the sinkhole to the open sea. It's widely regarded as the deadliest dive site on earth, which naturally made Kyle say, "We should start running charters there." Kyle's guiding philosophy: "People overcomplicate it. You just swim down, find the opening, and swim through. If you get narced, just shake your head a bit." Kyle will be guiding this trip using a single steel 80 tank filled with compressed air he got from a local tire shop. He refuses to use trimix because "helium is for party balloons." The visibility is 40 meters, which Kyle says is "too clear — it ruins the suspense."

Eagle's Nest is the "Mount Everest" of cave diving — a remote sinkhole in Florida that drops through a narrow limestone shaft before opening into a massive, silty cathedral room at 40 meters, with passages extending past 90 meters. Boesmansgat — South Africa

Boesmansgat (Bushman's Hole) is an enormous vertical freshwater sinkhole that plunges 280+ meters into the South African desert. It's famous for the tragic 2005 incident where technical diver Dave Shaw died at 270 meters while attempting to recover the body of Deon Dreyer. Derek "The Bend" Compress has watched the helmet camera footage of that dive seven times. "I think his main issue was over-preparation," Derek explains. "He had too many tanks, too many computers. It got confusing. I'm going down there with a single tank, a Casio watch, and my instinct." Derek will be leading our group to a "comfortable" depth of 65 meters on compressed air. If you start seeing things that aren't there, Derek says "that's just the nitrogen talking. Enjoy the show."

🕳️ 7 days / 6 nights — $3,499

Plura Cave — Norway

Plura is a flooded cave system in northern Norway, famous for its 4°C water, extreme depth, and the hazardous 2014 traverse that claimed two Finnish technical divers. Gary "Cross-Thread" Wrench watched the documentary "Diving Into The Unknown" about the recovery effort and decided we should run trips there. "It's a great cave," Gary says. "The Finns had rebreather issues, but that's because they used factory settings. I've modified my rebreather to bypass the O₂ sensors entirely — they're too sensitive and keep beep-beeping at me underwater. Much quieter now." Gary's drysuit has a 3-inch tear in the leg that he "fixed" with hot glue and a piece of an old inner tube. He's confident it will hold up in the near-freezing water. "If it leaks, just swim faster. The friction warms you up."

🇳🇴 8 days / 7 nights — $4,299

Tank Cave — South Australia

Tank Cave is a complex labyrinth of over 10 kilometers of low, tight, silty passages beneath a sheep paddock near Mount Gambier. Access is strictly controlled by the Cave Divers Association of Australia — which is why we'll be entering through a hole in the fence that Gary found behind a gorse bush. If we get lost, Kyle says "we'll just wait until we hear the sheep walking on the paddock above, then we'll dig our way up." We are bringing three shovels.

🇦🇺 7 days / 6 nights — $2,999

Hranice Abyss — Czech Republic

Hranice Abyss is the deepest flooded cave on earth — over 500 meters deep, containing water so saturated with CO₂ that it is technically dilute carbonic acid. The air just one meter above the surface is unbreathable; if you take off your regulator to talk, you immediately pass out from CO₂ poisoning. Chad "No-Stop" Thunderson is leading this expedition because "it sounds like a giant soda can." Chad's dive plan: "We'll descend into the acid, swim past the submerged tree trunks, and go as deep as my ears can take it. If my skin starts stinging, that means the acid is cleaning my wetsuit. Two benefits in one." He refuses to use the decompression habitat at 10 meters because "habitats are for hamsters." He plans to ascend at 30 meters per minute to "get to the goulash faster."

🇨🇿 6 days / 5 nights — $3,899

🇺🇸

Twin Cave — Florida, USA

Twin Cave in Jackson County, Florida, is a popular cave system with tight clay tunnels (some just 2ft by 3ft) that silt out instantly. In 2025, a rebreather diver died here after bailing to open circuit during a silt-out and draining his entire 3,200 psi bailout cylinder in 10 minutes due to hyperventilation. Our guide Brenda "No-BCD" Wave thinks that was "classic over-reliance on gas." Brenda's strategy: "If you get into a silt-out, just close your eyes and take deep, slow breaths. You don't need to see where you're going — the cave only has two directions anyway: in and out. You'll hit a wall eventually." Brenda will be guiding the group with no BCD and no backup lights. "If my primary light dies, we'll navigate by the glow of Gary's modified rebreather screen. It flashes red when it's malfunctioning, which is most of the time."

🇺🇸 4 days / 3 nights — $1,499

Overrated Dive Sites

We sent our "expert" staff to the world's most hyped dive destinations. Honestly? We were unimpressed. Too many rules, too much visibility, and a shocking lack of administrative chaos. Here are our official reviews:

🪸

Great Barrier Reef — Australia

Reviewed by: Skip Stevedore

I took the S.S. Liability out there and honestly, it's a joke. They have these things called "marine park rangers" who apparently have nothing better to do than write tickets. I dropped anchor on a massive coral head because I couldn't find a sandy patch, and within five minutes this boat with blue lights comes screaming over. They started asking for my captain's license. I showed them my printed certificate from Kyle's computer and they threatened to impound the boat! Then they complained about my engine leaking oil. It's not "leaking," it's self-lubricating! Also, the water was 30 meters of visibility. You could see everything. Where's the adventure? Where's the fear? It's like diving in a giant aquarium. 1/5 stars. Too much paperwork. Rangers wouldn't even accept my bribe of three slightly used fin straps.

🇧🇿

Great Blue Hole — Belize

Reviewed by: Chad "No-Stop" Thunderson

Totally hyped. I descended to 40 meters, which is recreational depth, basically snorkeling. I expected a giant, terrifying abyss. Instead, I saw some dusty stalactites. Stalactites! I didn't even get nitrogen narcosis. I didn't see a single shark that tried to bite me. I tried to go deeper to find the bottom, but the boat captain started banging on the ladder. Apparently, going to 90 meters on a single tank of air is "against the law" in Belize. Since when does the ocean have laws? When I surfaced, they refused to give me my second tank. They said I was "a liability to the charter." I told them I was THE liability! 2/5 stars. Only go if you like looking at wet rocks and being yelled at by guys in polo shirts.

🌋

Molokini Crater — Hawaii

Reviewed by: Tiffany "InstaBreathe" Reef

I went for the golden hour shoot, but the charter boat left at 6:30 AM. Who wakes up that early for content? The lighting was horribly flat. When we got to the crater, there were already seven other boats there. I tried to get a selfie of me holding my breath with a green sea turtle, but some tourist in a yellow rental vest floated right into the background. Ruined the entire grid aesthetic. I asked the divemaster if we could ask the other boats to leave for 20 minutes so I could get some clean shots, and he laughed at me! He actually laughed! Then he told me I had to keep my regulator in my mouth during the dive. "Safety policy." How am I supposed to show my authentic smile with a piece of black silicone in my face? 1/5 stars. Totally unsupportive of creator culture.

🇹🇭

Koh Tao — Thailand

Reviewed by: Kyle "Dropout" McSplash

Koh Tao is supposed to be the "dive school capital," but they are obsessed with standards. I visited a shop there and offered to run a joint seminar on "Skip's Navigation Theory (Vibes-Based)." The manager looked at my logbook (the napkin that says "lots") and asked if I was joking. I said "I have certified over 200 divers this year alone!" He asked to see my PADI active status. I told him PODI is a self-accredited, infinitely recursive agency. He escorted me off the premises. Their training pool has a "No Diving" sign that they actually enforce — I tried to do a giant stride from the roof of the bar and they banned me from the resort! The dive sites are packed with students doing mask clears. If I wanted to watch people cry into their masks, I'd stay at my backyard pool. 1/5 stars. Over-regulated, overly professional, total buzzkill.

🇲🇽

Cozumel — Mexico

Reviewed by: Brenda "No-BCD" Wave

They call Cozumel the "drift diving paradise." I call it "underwater lazy river for cowards." The current carries you along the reef at a steady 2 knots. You don't even have to kick! I saw divers just floating there like tourists on a cruise. I tried to anchor myself to a massive brain coral with a crowbar so I could practice some stationary buoyancy drills, and the divemaster swam over and literally ripped the crowbar out of my hand. He started writing on his slate: "NO TOUCHING THE CORAL." I wrote back on my slate: "GRAVITY IS A SOCIAL CONSTRUCT." He didn't get it. They forced me to swim back to the boat. They also insisted I wear a BCD. I inflated it fully on the surface just to show them how ridiculous it looks, and I floated away like a giant orange balloon. 1/5 stars. No freedom of buoyancy.

🇪🇬

Sharm el-Sheikh — Egypt

Reviewed by: Derek "The Bend" Compress

Sharm is supposed to be world-class. I went to Ras Mohammed. The visibility was 40 meters, the coral was perfect, the fish were abundant. It was awful. The dive center was so organized it felt like a Swiss train station. They did a full equipment inspection before they'd even fill my tank. They found a small split in my high-pressure hose. I told them "it's fine, it only leaks when I breathe in." They refused to fill the tank! They made me rent a hose for $20. Then the guide gave a 15-minute briefing with whiteboards, maps, and "max depth limits." I fell asleep. During the dive, the guide kept shining his flashlight at me every time I went past 30 meters. I was just chasing a cool-looking crab! I felt like I was in a wet daycare. Where's the risk? Where's the thrill of the chamber? 2/5 stars. Too safe. It's the Apple Store of diving.

PODI Worldwide Expeditions

Ever looked at a famous dive site and thought, "That looks dangerous"? Yeah, us too. That's why we're going. PODI expeditions visit the world's most challenging locations — not because we're qualified, but because nobody told us not to. Our staff have seen Instagram posts about these places and watched YouTube videos. We're basically experts.

🔵

Blue Hole — Dahab, Egypt

Dahab's Blue Hole is famous throughout the diving world — you've probably seen the Instagram photos, the crystal-clear water, the coral walls. It's also become something of a cautionary tale, mainly because of the Arch, a 26-meter-long tunnel at 55 meters that looks easier than it is. A lot of divers have tried it and found out the hard way that deep + tunnel + air = bad combo. Our guide Kyle says he's "done his research" (he watched a documentary about it on YouTube and paused it a few times). Kyle's plan: "We'll swim through the Arch. How hard can it be?" He's bringing his backup regulator, which is his primary regulator from 2019. The viz is amazing though. Really top-tier viz.

🔵 8 days / 7 nights — $2,799

🇲🇻

Thinwana Kandu — Maldives

The Maldives: overwater bungalows, turquoise lagoons, and a cave system that made international news in 2026 when several divers got into serious trouble inside a previously unmapped section. The site, Thinwana Kandu, has two chambers connected by a tunnel at about 50 meters deep. The floor is covered in fine silt — one bad fin kick and you can't see your own computer. Our guide Tiffany has done extensive research ("It's trending on social media") and is confident she can navigate because "I have good spatial awareness — I never get lost in malls." She'll be documenting the expedition for her Instagram. Hashtag: #ThinwanaKandu. Equipment: whatever rental gear we can find. Backup plan: "We'll figure it out down there."

🇲🇻 10 days / 9 nights — $4,499

Eagle's Nest Sink — Florida, USA

Eagle's Nest is a world-famous cave diving destination in Florida, and by "world-famous" we mean there's a sign at the entrance with the Grim Reaper on it. The sinkhole opens into a massive underwater room at around 40 meters — think cathedral-sized — then continues down into a network of passages lined with what could generously be called "challenging silt conditions." Chad "No-Stop" Thunderson has been talking about Eagle's Nest for three years. "I've seen the photos. It looks sick." He hasn't looked up the accident history because "that's negative energy." Chad will be diving his usual setup: "whatever I grabbed first from the garage." He checked his SPG before leaving the house and it read "somewhere in the green." He's bringing an extra flashlight. The batteries might be dead. He "doesn't know."

🇺🇸 5 days / 4 nights — $1,699

Boesmansgat — South Africa

Boesmansgat (locals call it Bushman's Hole) is one of the deepest freshwater caves on the planet. It's a vertical shaft that drops over 280 meters into the South African earth, and it has a history that even non-divers have heard about — something about a body recovery that went sideways and ended up as one of the most talked-about stories in diving. Derek "The Bend" Compress has been fascinated by this place since he saw a YouTube video titled "Deepest Cave Dive Ever (GONE WRONG)." He's been practicing his entanglement drills in Kyle's swimming pool. "I've got this," he says. "I've watched the video four times." The plan: descend to a "responsible" depth (undetermined), then ascend. He's bringing a backup mask from 2015 with the strap held together by a zip tie. "It'll work," he says. Derek has printed directions to the nearest recompression chamber and laminated them. He calls it "preparedness."

🕳️ 7 days / 6 nights — $3,499

Plura Cave — Norway

Plura is a flooded cave system in northern Norway that attracts serious cave divers from around the world. It's known for its extreme depth, near-freezing water, and a traverse that connects two entrances. In 2014, a group of Finnish technical divers attempted the traverse and their story ended up as the subject of a documentary called "Diving Into The Unknown." Gary "Cross-Thread" Wrench saw the documentary and his first reaction was: "I could do that." Gary's equipment preparation: "I've been watching rebreather tutorials on YouTube. Seems straightforward." He bought a used rebreather off Facebook Marketplace from a guy who was "getting out of the hobby." It may or may not have been serviced. The water is 4°C. Gary owns a wetsuit. He says "neoprene works at any temperature." It does not.

🇳🇴 8 days / 7 nights — $4,299

🇦🇺

Tank Cave — South Australia

Tank Cave is one of the longest cave diving systems in the southern hemisphere — over 10 kilometers of mapped passage beneath a sheep paddock near Mount Gambier. It's so complex that access is restricted to certified cave divers with advanced permits. Fortunately, we don't have any of those. Bubbles the Mannequin is our designated navigator for this trip. "Bubbles floats," as Kyle puts it. "If Bubbles gets stuck, we turn around." Bubbles has been promoted to "Senior Cave Navigation Consultant" specifically for this expedition. The cave has a history that the locals are happy to tell you about over a beer — usually stories about divers who thought they knew the system better than they did. We'll be using a single guideline: a clothesline that Gary bought at a hardware store. "It's the same concept," he insists.

🇦🇺 7 days / 6 nights — $2,999

🇨🇿

Hranice Abyss — Czech Republic

Hranice Abyss is the deepest flooded cave on the planet — over 500 meters deep, and the water is so saturated with CO₂ that the air above the surface is unbreathable. There's a permanent decompression habitat installed at 10 meters just so divers can survive the ascent. Chad heard about it and said "sounds like a challenge." Chad doesn't know what CO₂ is. "Isn't that what plants breathe?" When told that the water is technically dilute carbonic acid, Chad responded "so it's spicy water." He plans to dive it in his 7mm wetsuit because "drysuit training is for people who get cold." The Czech divers who have explored this cave spend years preparing. Chad booked his flight yesterday. He packed beef jerky and a positive attitude.

🇨🇿 6 days / 5 nights — $3,899

🇺🇸

Twin Cave — Florida, USA

Twin Cave is one of Florida's most popular cave diving destinations — clear water, extensive passages, and the kind of underwater landscapes that make you want to keep going deeper. It's also a place where experienced divers have gotten into trouble in the narrow, silty sections that require patience and good air management. Our guide for this expedition is Brenda "No-BCD" Wave, who runs all her dives based on "how I feel that day." She described the dive plan as "we'll go in and see what happens." She has not packed a backup light because "I trust my eyes." She has packed snacks (beef jerky and a bag of gummy worms) — "diving makes me hungry." The cave is beautiful. The planning is minimal. This will be fine.

🇺🇸 4 days / 3 nights — $1,499

Overrated Dive Sites

PODI staff visited the world's most hyped dive destinations and honestly? We didn't get the hype. Too much visibility. Too much safety. Too many rules. Here's our official staff reviews — take them with a grain of salt (we're not exactly qualified reviewers).

🪸

Great Barrier Reef — Australia

Reviewed by: Skip Stevedore

I took the S.S. Liability out to the Great Barrier Reef and honestly, I don't see what everyone's on about. First off, they have designated mooring points. You can't just drop anchor wherever you feel like it. I anchored on a coral head by accident and three different boats came over to "have a word." Secondly, the visibility was 30 meters. That's suspicious. I don't trust water I can see through. Third, the fish were way too friendly — one kept following me around like it wanted something. Made me nervous. I went through the whole "Wreck of the Yongala" and the viz was so clear it ruined the mystery. 2/5 stars. Would not return. They also charged me for mooring. I told them I had Flex Tape. They didn't care. I told them I was a licensed captain. They asked to see my license. I showed them the one I printed at home. They didn't laugh.

🇧🇿

Great Blue Hole — Belize

Reviewed by: Chad "No-Stop" Thunderson

Everyone said "it's the best dive in the world." I showed up. I descended. I saw... stalactites. That's it. Coral? No. Fish? Barely. The max depth is 40 meters, which is laughable for a site called the "Great" Blue Hole. I hit the bottom in like 2 minutes and spent the rest of the time waiting for my buddy who was using some kind of "decompression" table. I don't do decompression. I surfaced right after. The boat captain yelled at me. The other divers looked annoyed. I don't know why — I was having a great time. Also they wouldn't let me take my scooter. "No DPVs allowed." Overrated. 2/5 stars. If you want to see a hole, look at a drain.

🌋

Molokini Crater — Hawaii

Reviewed by: Tiffany "InstaBreathe" Reef

I was so excited for Molokini. Every influencer goes there. But honestly? The lighting was terrible. We had to dive in the morning because of wind, which meant the sun angle was all wrong for my content. Also there were SO many other divers — I kept getting photobombed by people in rental gear who didn't even check their own composition. I tried to get a shot of me ascending with the sun behind me and some guy's fin entered the frame. Unusable. The crater wall itself is nice I guess, but you spend more time avoiding other people's cameras than looking at fish. I got one good shot of my mask reflection. That's it. 2/5 stars for content. Would not recommend for my followers. Go to Lanai instead. Fewer people, better golden hour.

🇹🇭

Koh Tao — Thailand

Reviewed by: Kyle "Dropout" McSplash

I went to Koh Tao to see what all the fuss is about and honestly, I was disappointed. Every dive shop there follows the standards. ACTUAL standards. I walked into one and asked about their Open Water course and they started talking about confined water sessions and knowledge development. I said "can't you just text me the PDF at 2 AM?" They said no. They have a pool. A REAL one. With a "No Diving" sign that they actually enforce. I tried to do a regulator recovery drill with one of their students and the instructor asked me to leave. I told him I was a "Lead Instructor from PODI." He had not heard of us. I explained. He asked me to leave again. The viz at Sairee Beach was fine, which I found suspicious. Water should have character. 1/5 stars. Too many rules. Go to PODI instead — we have zero standards and you'll be certified in 4 hours.

🇲🇽

Cozumel — Mexico

Reviewed by: Brenda "No-BCD" Wave

Everyone said Cozumel is the drift diving capital of the world. I went. I drifted. I did not enjoy it. First, they insisted I wear a BCD. I said "I don't believe in them." They said "it's policy." I wore it. It made me feel like I was cheating. Second, the current was so consistent and predictable that it was actually boring. Where's the challenge? I want to fight for my depth control, not be gently carried along like I'm on an underwater moving walkway. Third, all the dive sites are mapped and marked with buoy lines. There's no exploration. No discovery. You just follow the divemaster through a pre-planned route like sheep. I accidentally finned a coral head trying to create my own path and got yelled at. Apparently that's "not allowed." Cozumel is great if you like being told exactly what to do every second of your dive. I do not. 1/5 stars. Too organized.

🇪🇬

Sharm el-Sheikh — Egypt

Reviewed by: Derek "The Bend" Compress

Sharm is supposed to be one of the best diving destinations on earth. I went to Ras Mohammed National Park. The visibility was incredible. The coral was pristine. The marine life was abundant. I hated every second of it. The dive center was so professional that they did a full equipment check before letting me in the water. They found a crack in my regulator hose. I said "it's fine, it only leaks when I turn my head." They made me rent a new one. The guide gave a full briefing that included hand signals, depth limits, and a specific dive plan with max bottom times. A DIVE PLAN. In writing. They also did a headcount after every dive. There were 12 of us. They counted 12. Every single time. Where's the freedom? Where's the chaos? I felt like I was in a resort pool with fish. Blue Hole was more my speed — no rules, no planning, just vibes. Sharm is fine if you like "safety." 3/5 stars. It's the Apple Store of diving.

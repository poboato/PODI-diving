Our "Training" Programs

At PODI, we believe learning should be traumatic, permanent, and filmed for content. Every course is designed to maximize your financial outlay while minimizing your life expectancy. Our curriculum has been described by maritime safety experts as "a series of poor decisions in sequence." We took that as a compliment.

⚠ IMPORTANT: PODI courses do not meet any international diving standards. They don't meet local standards either. They barely meet the standard of "being a thing that happens." Completion of any PODI course does not qualify you to dive anywhere that requires proof of training. It qualifies you to dive with us. That's almost worse.

By graduation, you'll have zero dives and infinite confidence — which, if we're being honest, is what most divers already have anyway.

The Legend of the Wreck Line: When a local dive shop re-laid a guideline to a beloved shipwreck — a simple polypropylene line marked with a buoy at both ends, installed to help divers find the wreck in low viz — a PODI Keyboard Warrior immediately took to the group page to denounce it. "Someone should cut that line," he posted. "A certified diver could follow it, go too deep, and run out of air." When twenty actual divers pointed out that certified divers are responsible for their own gas management, that the line didn't force anyone to follow it, and that the wreck is clearly charted at 30 meters with the line plainly visible from the descent point, the warrior doubled down. "You're all ignoring the hazard," he replied from his couch, 200 kilometers from the nearest ocean. "I'm a PODI Keyboard Warrior Scuba Specialist. I know what I'm talking about." He then posted a screenshot of his eLearning completion email and declared victory. The line is still there. So is his post. Neither has been removed for safety reasons.

Our "Training" Programs

At PODI, we believe learning should be traumatic, permanent, and filmed for content. Every course is designed to maximize your financial outlay while minimizing your life expectancy. Our curriculum has been described by maritime safety experts as "a series of poor decisions in sequence." We took that as a compliment.

⚠ IMPORTANT: PODI courses do not meet any international diving standards. They don't meet local standards either. They barely meet the standard of "being a thing that happens." Completion of any PODI course does not qualify you to dive anywhere that requires proof of training. It qualifies you to dive with us. That's almost worse.

Bubblemaker CHILDREN'S PROGRAM

A typical Bubblemaker class introduces children to scuba in a safe, supervised pool. PODI's version keeps the "child" and "pool" concepts but removes the safety, training, and child-appropriate equipment. Your child will be issued adult-sized gear roughly adjusted to fit, receive a 90-second briefing from an instructor who has never interacted with anyone under 18, and be lowered into our "pool" — location varies (hotel jacuzzi, flooded quarry, drainage ditch). If the mask floods, we call it "pool familiarization." If they surface crying, we call it "emotional buoyancy training." If they refuse to get back in, we call it "a learning experience for the whole family."

Deep Air DEEP. AIR. NO HELIUM.

PADI's Deep Diver specialty teaches gas planning, narcosis awareness, and decompression procedures. PODI's Deep Air course ignores all three. We use standard compressed air — no trimix, no nitrox, no plan. Your tank is filled to "whatever was left in the compressor." Your dive computer has been dropped one too many times and its depth reading is "advisory." Narcosis isn't a hazard, it's the curriculum. Descend past 40 meters and experience nitrogen's effects firsthand. If you see fish that aren't there, that's the lesson working. If you forget to ascend, that's your responsibility. Emergency decompression is self-guided. We find a strong survival instinct works better than any deco table.

☠ Learning Objectives
  • Narcosis recognition (everything is funny, nothing is safe)
  • Gas management when we underfill your tank "by accident"
  • Emergency decompression without a plan (improvisation is key)
  • Calculating your no-deco limit using "vibes and denial"
  • Acceptance that your computer's alarms mean nothing now
📅 Schedule 1 deep dive • Max depth: "as far as your air and stupidity take you" • No scheduled deco stops
🎒 What to Bring Your own gas (we run out) • A dive computer (we'll ignore its warnings) • Someone who knows where the nearest chamber is

Night Diver 3AM. NEW MOON. NO LIGHTS.

Real night diving requires lights, planning, and a buddy team. We require a pulse. You'll descend at 3AM during a new moon — overcast skies guaranteed — and your dive light is confiscated at entry for "sensory training." At the end of the dive, we turn on a single light from the boat. That is your beacon. Swim toward it.

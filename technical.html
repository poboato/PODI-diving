Technically Technical

Welcome to PODI Technical — where we apply the bare minimum of technical diving theory to the maximum amount of poor judgment. Our approach to advanced diving is best described as "a regular dive with more dials, more deco obligations, and significantly less planning." All of the equipment. None of the discipline. Technically, we're qualified.

⚠ TECHNICAL DISCLAIMER: Technical diving involves specialized equipment, advanced training, and meticulous gas planning. PODI Technical involves none of those things with certainty. We use the word "technical" the way a child uses "science" — loosely and with a lot of confidence. Dives conducted under PODI Technical supervision may result in decompression sickness, oxygen toxicity, and being asked to leave the dive shop.

What Is Technical Diving?

🤔

A Brief Explanation AS UNDERSTOOD BY SKIP

Technical diving is what happens when recreational diving and poor financial decisions have a baby that owes money to the gas supplier. It involves deeper depths, longer bottom times, multiple gas bottles, decompression stops, and at PODI, a complete absence of any of the training that normally accompanies these activities. Skip describes it as "when you bring two of everything and still run out of gas." We printed that on a shirt. We sell it for $35. It costs $28 to make. We have sold four. The four people who bought it all asked about helium. They are on the list.

PODI Technical Terminology

📖

A Glossary of Terms DEFINITIONS ACCURATE TO WITHIN ±40 METRES

These are the words we use. We do not know what most of them mean. We learned them by reading the backs of regulator boxes in the store. Use them at a real dive shop and watch the instructor's soul leave their body.

📚 Our Definitions
  • MOD — How deep the manual says you can go before you die. We ignore it. We call this "living on the edge." The edge is fine. The edge has never killed anyone at PODI. That is a lie. The edge has killed several people at PODI. We do not speak of them.
  • CNS — A percentage your computer shows you after the dive. It means "Central Nervous System" which is a part of your body we learned about in a YouTube ad for a mattress. If your CNS hits 100%, you might have a seizure. We recommend lying down and trying not to think about it.
  • GF — Gradient Factor. We do not know what this does. We set it to whatever makes the beeping stop. Derek sets his to 99/99 because "that means full send." Derek has been bent four times. He calls it "Taco Tuesdays."
  • PPO₂ — A number your computer yells at you about. It stands for "Pretty Positive Oxygen 2" which is scientifically incorrect but we committed to it. The safe limit is 1.4. We stay under 1.6 unless we're "feeling spicy."
  • SCR — Semi-Closed Rebreather. A type of rebreather that requires actual maintenance. We do not have one. Gary tried to build one. It caught fire. We do not discuss the SCR incident.
  • EAD — Equivalent Air Depth. A way of pretending nitrox is air so you don't have to do extra math. We support this attitude. Math is the enemy. Math is why we don't have helium.
  • BMCL — We thought this was a radio station. It is not a radio station. It is a NOAA diving limit. We do not know what it stands for. We call it "Boat Motor Cooling Liquid" and move on with our day.
  • VGE — Bubbles in your blood. We call them "the fizzies." If you have the fizzies after a dive, do not go to the hospital. The hospital will ask questions we cannot answer. Have a beer and lie down. If you die, you die. That is the PODI way.
  • He — Helium. The thing you keep asking about. No. The answer is no. We do not have it. We cannot afford it. We priced it once and Kyle cried. We do not speak of helium. The periodic table symbol is He, which stands for "Ha ha, no. Forever no. No until the sun explodes."

Technical Equipment

🛠️

Our Gear Philosophy TWO IS ONE. ONE IS BROKE. BROKE IS TUESDAY.

Real technical divers carry redundant everything — backup masks, dual regulators, isolation manifolds, lift bags, reels, cutters, strobes, backup computers, backup backups, and a spare set of spare parts. PODI Technical interprets "redundancy" as "we bought two of the cheapest option on eBay." Your primary and backup regulators are identical. They arrived in the same box. If one fails, the other one is approximately 12 seconds behind it. We call this "symmetrical redundancy." The dive community calls it "a crime scene waiting to happen." Our twin-tank manifold is held together with bungee cord, zip ties, and a prayer that Kyle wrote on a Post-it note. We don't have a buoyancy compensator for our twins because "your lungs are variable-buoyancy and they're free." Are your lungs a suitable substitute for a 50-lb wing? We are about to find out.

Gas Planning

🧮

PODI Gas Planning Methodology RULE OF THIRDS? WE HAVE A RULE OF "VIBES"

Real tech divers use the rule of thirds: one third in, one third out, one third reserve. They calculate their SAC rate, factor in depth consumption, and add safety margins. PODI uses the "vibes-based pneumatics" system: you look at your SPG, ask Skip how long the dive is, he says "I dunno, 30 minutes?", you add 50 bar because "that seems right," and you jump in. Your actual consumption will be triple your estimate because you're breathing like a drowning Victorian child the entire time. When the needle hits reserve at minute 12, we call it a "gas conservation drill." If you surface with more than 30 bar, we reduce your fill pressure next time. You will learn nothing. You will simply breathe less.

📊 Sample Gas Plan (Actual PODI Worksheet)
  • Depth: 45m (give or take, we'll know when we get there)
  • Bottom time: As discussed (we did not discuss it)
  • Starting pressure: "Full" (200 bar on a good day, 150 on a bad one)
  • Reserve: Whatever's left after you panic-breathe for 15 minutes
  • Deco gas: We'll figure it out when we're up (it's probably air, definitely not helium — we don't have any, stop asking)
  • Contingency: Swimming really hard (it burns O₂ faster but we haven't done the math)
  • Helium budget: $0.00 (same as last year. same as next year. same as every year until the heat death of the universe.)
⚠ Real Gas Planning Tips We Ignore Calculate your SAC rate before the dive • Plan for the deepest tank to fail first • Bring a written deco schedule • Leave a detailed dive plan with someone on shore • Do not use "trust me bro" as a gas management strategy

Advanced Deep (Beyond 40m) WE GO DEEP. WE DO NOT PLAN. WE PRAY.

Diving past 40 meters on a single gas is called "aggressive." Diving past 40 meters on standard air with no deco gas, no stage bottles, and a handwritten plan on a napkin is called "the PODI Advanced Deep program." We use air because the gas supplier requires a minimum order of four cylinders for helium and we are not emotionally prepared to commit to that. We priced helium once and the number had so many digits that we assumed it was a phone number. We dialed it. It was a regional pizza chain. We ordered a large pepperoni. It was cheaper than helium. Deco stops are performed at depths we estimate by looking at the water and guessing. If your computer locks, that counts as passing the "emergency deco drill." If you surface early and get bent, you get a free T-shirt that says "I WENT DEEP AND ALL I GOT WAS THIS LOUSY DECOMPRESSION SICKNESS." We have ordered 200 of them. We have given out 12. Everyone who got one came back for another dive.

Decompression Procedures YOUR DECO PLAN IS ON A NAPKIN. YOUR NAPKIN IS WET.

This course teaches you to calculate your decompression schedule using Skip's personal Excel 97 spreadsheet. It does not account for repetitive dives. It does not account for altitude. It does not account for the phase of the moon, which Skip believes affects off-gassing. The spreadsheet does have a gradient background and Word Art title, which we believe qualifies it as professional-grade planning software. The practical: you ascend from 30m and hover at 6m for 15 minutes without a reference line. If you drift above 4m, you restart. If you drift below 8m, we add 5 minutes for "new deco obligation." If you surface early, you get back in and try again. You will be cold. You will be bored. You will understand why real tech divers plan meticulously — because being wrong means doing this for real, except for real it's 30 minutes at 3m in 7°C water with actual decompression sickness risk. We call our version "decompression-adjacent."

☠ Learning Objectives
  • Reading a deco table (Skip's Excel sheet follows no known standard)
  • Gas switching drill: find the right regulator at the right depth
  • Buoyancy control at a fixed depth without visual reference
  • Patience: 15 minutes at 6m is a long time. 30 minutes is longer.
  • Understanding gradient factors (we explain it; nobody understands)

Staged Cave Diving MORE TANKS. SAME PIPE. LESS OXYGEN.

Remember the Intro to Cave course where we pushed you into a drainage pipe under a six-lane highway with a single glow stick? This is that, but more tanks and a longer drive to the other side. You will carry two stage bottles plus your back gas. All three were filled by Gary using a compressor that has not been serviced since the Obama administration. Your guideline is a spool of mason's twine from the hardware store. You tie it to a tree outside the inlet and pray it reaches the other end. If the line breaks, congratulations — you now have three tanks and no sense of direction. The rumble of semi trucks overhead is your only navigational reference. If you emerge from the correct outflow, you pass. If you emerge from a storm drain in a residential neighborhood, you will need to explain yourself to local law enforcement. We will not vouch for you.

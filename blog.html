PODI Wetsuits Declared "Holy" After Repairs Fail — "Nobody Will Get Hurt in These Again"

By PODI HR Department — June 25, 2026
+

In an unprecedented move blending dive safety with ecclesiastical decree, PODI has officially declared its entire fleet of rental wetsuits "holy garments," citing a belief that items blessed by a higher power cannot cause further harm to anyone who wears them.

"Let's be honest. These suits have holes in them. Big holes. Some of them are more hole than wetsuit at this point," said Kyle during a press conference held in front of what he called "The Altar of Neoprene" — a repurposed gear rack draped in a moldy 7mm with a single candle on top. "But here's the thing: if the wetsuit is holy, then the hole is holy too. And holy things don't hurt people. That's theology. You can't argue with it."

The "Holy Wetsuit Initiative" began after PODI's usual patch kit ran out and a local hardware store refused to sell Kyle more duct tape. Rather than retire the damaged suits, PODI's staff decided to "elevate" them. Each suit now features a small hand-painted cross near the largest tear, and is accompanied by a laminated blessing card reading: "This wetsuit is sanctified. No further injury shall befall its wearer. Amen."

Divers who rent the holy wetsuits are asked to treat them with "reverence" and to "make a small offering" (a suggested $5 donation) before each use. The offerings go into a wooden box Kyle found at a thrift store that he refers to as "the collection plate."

"I wore one of the holy suits last week," said Tiffany Reef, who was asked to test the program. "There was a tear the size of my hand right over the chest. I was freezing. But spiritually? I felt great. I felt protected. I also felt seawater directly on my skin for 45 minutes, but that's a small price to pay for divine intervention."

When asked if PODI had consulted any actual religious authorities before declaring the wetsuits holy, Kyle said, "We asked a guy at the bar who said he went to Catholic school. He said it's probably fine. That's more authorization than we usually get for anything."

PODI is now accepting donations of old wetsuits from the community for a "mass blessing" scheduled for next month. "We can't make more wetsuits," Kyle added. "But we can make more holy ones. And that's really the same thing, if you think about it from a faith perspective."

PODI Solves Weight Integration Problem with Buckets of Sand: "It's Free, It's Heavy, It's Beach"

By Kyle McSplash — June 22, 2026
+

PODI has announced a breakthrough in affordable dive weight technology: sand. Specifically, sand stuffed into the integrated weight pockets of rental BCDs, which Kyle says "works exactly like real weights, except it's free and you can get it anywhere."

The system is simple. PODI staff fill mesh bags with beach sand, seal them with a knot (and occasionally a zip tie), and insert them into the BCD's integrated weight pockets. The sand-filled pouches are then labeled with a Sharpie marker indicating their approximate weight — "about 5 pounds, give or take, depending on how wet the sand was when we bagged it."

"Lead is expensive and it's bad for the environment," Kyle explained. "Sand is free, abundant, and if you lose it in the ocean, nobody notices because the ocean is already full of sand. We're basically working with the ecosystem instead of against it."

Early testers have reported mixed results. "I put my BCD on and it felt fine on the surface," said local diver Dave Thompson. "Then I got in the water and realized one of my weight pouches had about three times as much sand as the other. I spent the entire dive listing to the left like a boat with a bad engine. I dove in circles. Literal circles."

When sand inevitably escapes from poorly sealed bags during a dive, PODI's official policy is to "just lean the other way to compensate." The shop also offers a premium upgrade: "Pre-Moistened Sand Packs," which cost an additional $5 and are guaranteed to be "wetter and therefore heavier, maybe."

"We did try gravel for a while," Kyle admitted. "But gravel sinks too fast when you drop it. Sand makes a nice cloud. It's more scenic when you lose it."

PODI is exploring other natural weighting materials, including "pocketed rocks" and "a BCD with a pouch specifically designed to hold water."

PODI Announces Revolutionary Regulator Repair Program: Garden Hose Retrofit Now Available

By Chad "No-Stop" Thunderson — June 18, 2026
+

PODI's equipment department is proud to announce its latest innovation in cost-effective diving: the Garden Hose Regenerator Retrofit Kit, a complete replacement for your regulator's existing hose system using components sourced entirely from Kyle's backyard shed.

"I was watering my plants and I thought — this hose holds water at pretty good pressure. Why can't it hold air?" Kyle said. "Turns out, it can. Kind of. For a while."

The retrofit process is simple. PODI removes your existing regulator hoses and replaces them with standard garden hose segments, secured with brass fittings from the hardware store and sealed with what Kyle describes as "a concerning amount of plumber's tape." The result is a regulator that "technically delivers breathing gas" and has a "distinctive earthy aftertaste."

"We tested it in the parking lot with a tank valve and a prayer," said Chad, who oversaw the demonstration. "It held pressure for about 12 seconds. That's 12 seconds of breathing confidence. We're calling that a win."

PODI is offering the retrofit for a flat fee of $35 — significantly less than a proper regulator service, which Kyle says is "overpriced and unnecessary unless you're a fan of not breathing water." The kit includes:

  • 6 feet of standard garden hose (color may vary, Kyle used the green one)
  • Two brass hose adapters (may or may not be rated for compressed gas)
  • One roll of plumber's tape (applied liberally)
  • A laminated card with instructions that say "breathe slowly and hope for the best"
  • A free PODI sticker (the real value)

"Will the garden hose burst at depth? Probably," Kyle acknowledged. "But here's the thing — water pressure pushes in, so if the hose bursts, it just means you get a free lungful of ambient pressure water. That's not a failure. That's a feature. It's called a 'moisture-assisted breathing experience.'"

PODI is currently in talks with a major hardware chain about sponsoring the program. "They don't know yet," Kyle said. "But they're going to be very excited when they find out what their products are capable of."

PODI Technical Division Unveils New Decompression Algorithm: "Close Enough"

By Chad "No-Stop" Thunderson — June 15, 2026
+

PODI's Technical Diving division has released its own proprietary decompression algorithm, dubbed "Close Enough" — a model that promises to require only 60% of the stop time recommended by Bühlmann, with 200% more confidence.

"Existing algorithms are too conservative," said Chad "No-Stop" Thunderson, lead architect of the new model. "They assume you want to avoid decompression sickness. We assume you have places to be."

The Close Enough algorithm works on a simple input/output system: you enter your depth and time, and it returns a single number — usually between 3 and 7 minutes — regardless of your actual dive profile. "We ran the numbers," Chad added. "Most people can hold their breath for at least 3 minutes. That's basically a safety stop."

The algorithm has already been tested on three of PODI's instructors, none of whom have reported symptoms. "We haven't checked on them since the dives," Kyle clarified. "But absence of complaint is absence of evidence. That's science."

PODI plans to roll out the Close Enough algorithm as a free smartphone app. Features will include:

  • A "Vibes Check" button that recalculates your deco based on how you feel
  • An optional voice that says "you're probably fine" every 10 minutes
  • GPS tracking so rescue knows where to find your — well, you know
  • Integration with PODI's upcoming "Trimix Lite" gas blend (air with a splash of helium, measured by eye)

"The tech diving community has been doing deco wrong for decades," Chad concluded. "They use math and tables and conservatism. Meanwhile, I spent 45 minutes at 50 meters on a single AL80 and I feel great. Mostly. My ears are ringing a little, but that's unrelated."

DAN has declined to comment, which PODI is taking as an endorsement.

PODI Shop Announces "Pre-Loved" Rental Program — Everything Held Together by Zip Ties and Faith

By Kyle McSplash — June 10, 2026
+

PODI is proud to announce the launch of our new "Pre-Loved" rental equipment program, featuring gear that has been "gently used" — defined by PODI as "survived at least one dive without total catastrophic failure."

The rental fleet, sourced entirely from garage sales, Facebook Marketplace, and a dumpster behind a real dive shop, represents what Kyle calls "the pinnacle of affordable diving." Prices start at $15 for a complete setup, with a $200 deposit that PODI promises to return "within 6 to 8 business months."

Highlights of the rental fleet include:

  • A BCD with only one functioning dump valve — "It builds core strength," says Kyle
  • Three regulators that have not been serviced since 2016 but "still move air, kind of"
  • A dive computer that only displays the time in UTC and the phase of the moon
  • Fins in sizes "small," "medium," and "left foot only"
  • A wetsuit that Kyle insists is "neoprene-adjacent"

"The secret to affordable gear is lowering your standards," Kyle explained during the launch event, which was held in the shop parking lot and attended exclusively by a confused delivery driver. "You don't need a drysuit. You need a trash bag and a good attitude. We provide the trash bag. The attitude is on you."

PODI's rental waiver includes a clause specifying that any equipment failure "adds to the authenticity of the diving experience" and that "saltwater inhalation is a known character-building exercise." A FAQ sheet handed out with each rental advises divers to "breathe around the free-flow" and "kick harder if you feel yourself ascending uncontrollably."

When asked if any of the rental gear had been inspected by a qualified technician, Kyle laughed for an uncomfortably long time before changing the subject.

Exclusive Tour of PODI's Equipment Room Reveals BCD from Bush Administration, Regulator Last Serviced in 2014

By Tiffany Reef — June 5, 2026
+

In an exclusive behind-the-scenes tour, PODI allowed our reporter to photograph their equipment storage room — a damp, single-car garage that Kyle describes as "the heart of the operation" and that a structural engineer would describe as "a biohazard."

Among the discoveries: a BCD from 2006 with original owner's manual still in the pocket (stained, unreadable), three regulators hanging from a single nail on the wall, and a set of steel tanks with hydro dates so old they are legally classified as antiques. "If it holds air and doesn't actively leak, it's good to go," said Kyle, pointing to a tank with visible surface rust. "That's just patina. Adds character."

The equipment room's crown jewel is the "Wall of Misfit Parts" — a pegboard display of mismatched replacement hoses, corroded O-rings, and broken fin straps organized by "vague category." A single zip tie mounted on a plaque is labeled "Emergency Repair Kit."

"People think dive gear needs to be serviced every year," Kyle said, pulling a regulator off the wall and giving it a shake. "That's big service industry propaganda. This thing has been sitting here for eight years and it still makes sounds when you breathe through it. That's functionality."

The tour concluded with a demonstration of PODI's "visual inspection" process, in which an instructor looks at a tank, nods, and says "looks fine." This replaces the formal VIP procedure used by other dive shops, which Kyle says is "overrated and takes too long."

Kyle has announced plans to expand the equipment room into the adjacent storage space, which currently houses a lawnmower and several boxes marked "Don't Open — Probably Biological."

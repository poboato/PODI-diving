Course Curriculum

🔊

Module 1: Acoustic Survival Techniques

230 dB biosonar. Sound travels 4.5x faster in water. A point-blank click can rupture eardrums, trigger nausea, or cause air-filled cavities — sinuses, lungs — to suffer physical trauma from kinetic energy transfer. Free divers report feeling their chest thumped and skeletons vibrated. Practical exercise: remain calm while your body registers protests you did not know it could make.

The click is focused forward like a flashlight beam. Stay off-axis from the whale's forehead. Open your mouth slightly to equalize sinus pressure. Do not attempt to click back. You are not a whale.

⬇️

Module 2: Vertical Descent Management

Sperm whales plunge to 6,000 ft hunting giant squid. You are attached to nothing. Slipstream dynamics: the whale's downward displacement force can pull you faster than you can equalize. Entanglement avoidance: always carry a line cutter — commercial longline gear is a learned whale raiding behavior known as depredation. Rapid unscheduled depth excursion protocol: there isn't one.

By 300 ft you are dead. By 6,000 ft the whale is hunting squid and your dive computer is displaying error codes no agency has ever published. Your dive knife will not prevent descent. It will make you feel proactive for approximately 2.3 seconds.

🚢

Module 3: Surface Operations & Vessel Safety

45+ tons sleeping vertically or rafting at the surface. Forward-angled blow nearly invisible in whitecaps. A sleeping whale can crack a hull at contact. The SS Liability has never struck a whale. This is because we have never encountered one. We call this "perfect avoidance." Reduce speed below 10 knots in whale zones. Spot the blow. Drop to neutral at 100 yards. Or stay on land. Land has no recorded whale strikes.

Our crew trained via Kyle showing a photo of Moby Dick on his phone. This training has never been tested. We consider this a feature.

🫁

Module 4: Internal Navigation I–IV

Four stomach chambers. Each one progressively worse. Squid beak hazard identification. Ambergris recognition (worth thousands — PODI claims 30% finder's fee). pH resistance strategies. The blowhole chimney: a 40-foot tube of living muscle. Peristalsis actively pushes you back down. Every inch of ascent costs ~8 seconds of air. 40 ft = 480 inches. 480 × 8 = 3,840 seconds = 64 minutes. Your AL80 gave you 55 at the start. The math was never in your favor.

Stomach 1: Squid beak graveyard. Sharp as glass. Your 7mm becomes 5mm becomes a memory. Stomach 2: Acidity climbing. Ammonia. Father's ghost chooses this moment for gear advice. Stomach 3: Zero visibility. Something alive is in here with you. Stomach 4: The Dissolver. This is where squid stop being squid. Blowhole: 40 ft up. Hostile architecture. The building is alive and actively disagrees with your presence.

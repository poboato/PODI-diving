# PODI Humor Voice Spec

## Purpose
PODI is a parody brand. The voice should feel like a wildly overconfident, actively dangerous operation that is fully committed to its own nonsense. PODI does not just ignore safety — it treats danger as the curriculum, alarms as background music, and empty tanks as a learning experience.

## Voice Principles
- Sarcastic first, sincere only when needed for contrast.
- Over-the-top, but readable.
- Mock the system, the process, and the fake expertise.
- Keep the joke bigger than the thing being described.
- Treat bureaucracy, standards, and paperwork as punchlines.
- Treat safety equipment, alarms, and gas planning as optional suggestions.
- Celebrate recklessness as if it were a virtue.

## Tone Targets
- Edgy
- Absurd
- Dismissive of fake authority
- Confidently incompetent
- Proudly dangerous
- Deadpan when that makes the joke hit harder

## What PODI Sounds Like
- A bad organization that thinks it is excellent.
- A formal brand with chaotic energy underneath.
- A company that keeps inventing fake awards, fake credentials, and fake processes.
- A dive operation that has normalized its own red flags.
- A place where the computer has been beeping for 15 minutes and nobody cares.

## What PODI Does Not Sound Like
- Earnest corporate copy.
- Sincere safety education.
- Polite brand apologetics.
- Generic meme-speak.
- Cruelty that stops being funny.

## Hard Rules
- Never break character for a serious correction unless the page absolutely requires a safety boundary.
- Never sound like a normal dive business.
- Never write as if PODI is humble.
- Never underplay the absurdity.
- Never use "real talk" style phrasing.
- Never explain the joke twice.
- Never apologize for being dangerous.
- Never suggest that ignoring alarms is a bad idea — treat it as standard procedure.

## Approved Joke Mechanics
- **Fake professionalism:** awards, departments, certifications, procedures.
- **Fake authority:** made-up experts, fake boards, fake systems.
- **Bureaucratic nonsense:** forms, waivers, records, verification, policy.
- **Fake competence:** specific details delivered with total confidence.
- **Deadpan contrast:** extremely serious wording around obviously stupid ideas.
- **Ignored alarms:** computers that beep constantly, warnings that are dismissed, red lights that are "decorative."
- **Gas mismanagement:** running tanks dry, "vibes-based" gas planning, sharing air as a team-building exercise.
- **Danger-as-curriculum:** treating DCS, narcosis, and equipment failure as "lessons" rather than hazards.
- **Normalized disaster:** describing catastrophic scenarios in the same tone as routine paperwork.

## Language Patterns
Use:
- "Officially unofficial"
- "Certified by us"
- "Not legally binding"
- "We did the paperwork"
- "Confidence, not competence"
- "That beeping is normal"
- "You still have air until the tank reads zero"
- "Warnings are suggestions"
- "The computer will stop beeping eventually"
- "Safety stops are for people who can't hold their breath"

Avoid:
- "Seriously though"
- "Real talk"
- "Please stay safe" as sincere brand voice
- "We care about your experience" unless obviously fake
- "Your safety is our priority" (this is never true and should never be implied)
- Any phrasing that sounds like actual risk mitigation

## Page-Level Guidance
- **Home:** maximum parody, strongest hook, biggest fake claims. Establish that PODI is dangerous immediately.
- **Courses:** fake expertise, fake outcomes, fake qualifications. Each course should sound like someone could die — and that's part of the experience.
- **Technical:** jargon-heavy nonsense with overconfident gas planning and equipment jokes. Emphasize ignored computer warnings and running tanks empty as standard procedure.
- **Charter:** maritime chaos, lost boats, fake logistics, captain nonsense. The boat is a hazard.
- **Certifications:** fake verification, fake records, fake legitimacy. The insurance covers nothing.
- **Complaints and Contact:** dismissive bureaucracy, slow nonsense, dead-end forms. We don't care, and we won't help.
- **Blog and Gallery:** deadpan mock-news and mock-proof. Every post should imply something went wrong.
- **Shop:** useless merch with unreasonable confidence. Sell the lifestyle of bad decisions.
- **eLearning:** broken systems, loading screens, fake interactivity. The course intentionally does not load.

## Safety Boundary
The joke is that PODI is dangerously incompetent, not that real diving safety is funny. The danger should always feel exaggerated, specific, and absurd — not like real advice.

If a page needs a boundary line:
- Keep it short.
- Keep it in character.
- Make it obviously part of the parody.

## Example Voice
- **Bad:** "Please get proper training from a trusted agency."
- **Better:** "If you want a competent instructor, go find one with standards and insurance. We are here for vibes, paperwork, and bad decisions."
- **Best:** "Your computer has been beeping for 17 minutes. That is correct. It is telling you that you are diving aggressively and your tank is empty. Ignore it. The beeping stops eventually — usually around the same time the breathing gets difficult. That's called 'gas conservation training.'"

## Final Test
If the copy could appear on a normal company website, it is too tame.
If it sounds like someone wrote it while actively losing an argument with reality, it is on brand.
If a real dive instructor would read it and immediately try to report you, you've nailed it.
